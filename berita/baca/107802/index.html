Ini Dia Tulang Punggung Manufaktur RI, Punya 6,9 Juta Tenaga Kerja

Redaksi LapakStore88 · diolah dari CNBC Indonesia

LokalStore88 Rabu,4 November 2026.

Nearly 6.9 million jobs hinge on a single sector that often goes unnoticed in daily economic discussions. This is not a minor industry; it is the backbone of Indonesian manufacturing. The food and beverage industry, often perceived as simple consumer goods, actually drives a massive portion of the nation's economic engine. Its impact is so profound that it shapes the country's trade balance and industrial growth trajectory significantly. Understanding this sector is crucial for anyone following Indonesia's economic future.

In the first semester of 2026, the contribution of this sector to the non-oil and gas processing GDP reached 41.86%. This figure illustrates that nearly half of the country's non-oil and gas manufacturing output comes from food and beverage products. Minister of Industry Sarmuji highlighted this statistic during the Krista Interfood Expo 2026 held in PIK 2. The data confirms that this industry is not just a supporting player but a primary pillar. It anchors the broader manufacturing landscape against external economic fluctuations.

The growth rate of this sector also exceeded expectations, expanding by 6.77% in the first half of the year. This performance is 0.68 percentage points higher than the same period in the previous year. Such consistent growth indicates strong demand and resilience within the domestic and export markets. Investors are taking notice, as evidenced by the flow of capital into this specific industrial segment. The sector's ability to outpace previous years' performance signals a healthy and expanding market environment.

Capital investment has continued to flow steadily into the food and beverage industry, reaching 47.74 trillion rupiah in the first semester. Notably, 59% of this investment originated from domestic sources, showcasing strong local confidence. This domestic capital injection is vital for building a sustainable industrial foundation that is less dependent on foreign volatility. The presence of robust local investment suggests that entrepreneurs believe in the long-term viability of the sector. It also means that economic benefits are retained within the local economy more effectively.

From a trade perspective, the performance of this sector has been exceptionally strong, particularly in balancing the national accounts. Exports of food and beverage products reached 34.65 billion US dollars between January and August 2026. In contrast, imports during the same period were recorded at approximately 9.7 billion US dollars. This disparity creates a substantial surplus in the trade balance of nearly 25 billion US dollars. Such a large surplus is a critical pillar for stabilizing the national currency and strengthening foreign exchange reserves.

The trade surplus generated by this industry is not merely a statistical footnote but a significant economic asset. Minister Sarmuji emphasized that overlooking the food and beverage sector would mean ignoring a major source of national revenue. The sheer volume of exports demonstrates Indonesia's capability to compete in global food markets. This sector acts as a shock absorber for the economy, providing stability when other commodity sectors face price swings. Its consistent performance ensures a steady flow of foreign currency into the country.

Strategic Role in National Economy

The strategic importance of the food and beverage industry extends beyond just financial contributions to the GDP. It serves as a dual engine for both devisa collection and massive employment generation. With 6.9 million workers employed, this sector is one of the largest employers in Indonesia. This scale of employment has a direct multiplier effect on household income and local consumption. A stable workforce in this sector translates to improved purchasing power across various regions of the country.

Furthermore, the industry plays a pivotal role in maintaining the overall performance of national manufacturing. By driving productivity and innovation, it sets a standard for other industrial sub-sectors to follow. The government recognizes this potential and is actively supporting the sector's development through various policies. These policies aim to ensure that the industry can continue to grow sustainably in the long term. The focus is on creating an ecosystem that encourages both domestic and international expansion.

Driving Productivity and Innovation

To sustain its growth, the industry must continually enhance its productivity and embrace technological innovations. Minister Sarmuji noted that the current achievements, while impressive, require further strengthening in these areas. Utilizing local raw materials is a key strategy to reduce costs and increase self-sufficiency. By relying on domestic inputs, the industry can insulate itself from global supply chain disruptions. This approach also supports upstream sectors such as agriculture and fisheries, creating a more integrated economy.

In addition to product innovation, the government is pushing for higher utilization rates of existing production capacities. Many factories in this sector still have significant unused capacity, which represents a missed economic opportunity. Maximizing this capacity can lead to increased output without the need for massive new infrastructure investments. This efficiency is crucial for maintaining competitiveness in the global market. It allows companies to respond more quickly to changing consumer demands and market trends.

The positive performance of the food and beverage sector is evident in multiple dimensions, including growth, investment, trade, and employment. These interconnected factors create a virtuous cycle that benefits the broader economy. Investors are encouraged by the stability and profitability of the sector, leading to further capital inflows. This confidence is essential for continuous industrial development and technological upgrades. The sector's success is a testament to the strength of Indonesia's consumer base and its export potential.

Strengthening the Supply Chain

Government efforts are also focused on strengthening the entire supply chain of the food and beverage industry from upstream to downstream. Indonesia boasts abundant raw materials from agriculture, plantations, and fisheries, which provide a natural competitive advantage. Leveraging these resources allows for greater production scale and market expansion. The goal is to transform these raw materials into higher-value processed goods. This value addition is critical for maximizing the economic return from natural resources.

However, the development of this industry cannot rely solely on selling finished products to final consumers. The government emphasizes the need to strengthen the processing industry to produce intermediate goods. These intermediate products can serve as inputs for other manufacturing processes, thereby broadening the industry's role in the value chain. By supplying other industries, food and beverage companies can secure more stable and long-term contracts. This diversification reduces risk and opens up new revenue streams beyond consumer retail.

This intermediate product strategy allows food and beverage businesses to play a wider role in the national economy. They are no longer just manufacturers of end-consumer goods but also suppliers of industrial inputs. This shift enhances their position in the global supply chain and increases their bargaining power. It also fosters stronger linkages between different industrial sectors, promoting a more cohesive and resilient economy. The long-term vision is to create a robust industrial ecosystem where each sector supports and strengthens the others.

Future Outlook and Conclusion

In conclusion, the food and beverage industry is undeniably the backbone of Indonesia's manufacturing sector. Its contribution to GDP, trade surplus, and employment makes it a critical pillar of national economic stability. The positive trends in growth and investment indicate a sector that is well-positioned for continued success. However, sustaining this momentum requires ongoing efforts in productivity, innovation, and supply chain integration. The government's focus on value addition and intermediate goods production is a smart strategic move.

As Indonesia continues to develop its industrial base, the role of the food and beverage sector will likely become even more pronounced. Its ability to adapt to changing market conditions and technological advancements will be key to its future success. Stakeholders across the industry must work together to address challenges and capitalize on opportunities. The next few years will be crucial for implementing the strategies that will secure the sector's dominance. A stronger food and beverage industry means a stronger and more resilient Indonesia.
