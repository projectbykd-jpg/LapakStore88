Kejagung Buka Lelang Batu Bara Sitaan Kasus Samin Tan

Redaksi LapakStore88 · diolah dari Liputan6 News

LokalStore88 Kamis,15 Oktober 2026.

In a move that sent ripples through Indonesia’s coal sector, the Supreme Court has opened a digital auction for coal assets worth more than Rp 263 billion. The sale involves 237,000 tons of coal seized from PT Asmin Koalindo Tuhup, a company embroiled in a corruption probe. The auction is conducted entirely online, allowing bidders from across the nation to participate in real time. With a reserve price set at Rp 263 080 879 000, the process is expected to draw significant attention from mining firms and investors alike. This unprecedented step illustrates the judiciary’s commitment to recovering assets tied to illegal activities.

Background

The seizure stems from allegations that PT Asmin Koalindo Tuhup, under the direction of Samin Tan, continued mining operations after its license was revoked in 2017. The Kejaksaan Agung’s Badan Pemulihan Aset (BPA) is responsible for managing the liquidation of such assets. The case, formally listed on March 27 2026, accused Tan of exploiting the company’s resources until 2025, bypassing regulatory oversight. The BPA’s decision to auction the coal reflects a broader strategy to deter corruption within the mining sector. By converting seized assets into marketable goods, authorities aim to offset the financial losses caused by illicit activities.

Assets and Location

The auction catalog includes 171,107 tons of coal stored at the jetty of PT AC and an additional 66,000 tons distributed across 15 barges. All consignments are situated in Desa Damparan, Dusun Hilir, Barito Selatan, a region known for its extensive coal deposits. Inspectors have verified the quality and quantity of the material, ensuring transparency for potential bidders. The concentration of assets in a single locality simplifies logistics but also raises questions about environmental oversight. Stakeholders will need to navigate both the opportunity and the responsibility associated with handling large-scale coal stocks.

Eligibility and Rules

Participation is limited to entities holding valid mining, transport, and sales permits, ensuring that only qualified operators can bid. Companies must also be registered under a valid Tax Identification Number, as mandated by the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources. This restriction aims to prevent unlicensed players from entering the market, thereby upholding industry standards. The Kejaksaan’s guidelines, issued under Ministerial Decision 162.K/HK.02/MEM.H/2026, specify that bidders must possess a legal framework supporting their operations. Non-compliant firms will automatically be disqualified, maintaining the integrity of the auction.

Online Bidding Process

Bidders are required to submit a bid guarantee of Rp 130 billion, a substantial figure that underscores the auction’s seriousness. The guarantee will be refunded if the bidder does not win the final bid, protecting legitimate participants from undue loss. The e-Auction platform lelang.go.id facilitates real-time bidding, allowing transparent monitoring by all parties. The platform’s open-bidding mechanism ensures that the highest bid wins, reflecting market dynamics. The digital format also reduces administrative overhead, making the process more efficient.

Timeline and Logistics

Bidding will close on October 28, 2026, at 14:00 WIB, aligning with the server time to avoid discrepancies. Prospective bidders will have the opportunity to inspect the assets on October 26, between 09:00 and 12:00, at PT Artha Contractors in Desa Damparan. This pre-auction tour provides insight into the condition of the coal and the surrounding infrastructure. After the auction, the winning party will receive the coal under terms outlined by the BPA. The entire process is designed to be swift, yet thorough, ensuring a fair distribution of assets.

Perpetrator Profile

Samin Tan, the alleged mastermind, was named as a suspect on March 27, 2026, in the corruption case against PT Asmin Koalindo Tuhup. Investigators claim he maintained mining operations despite the license revocation, exploiting loopholes to continue revenue generation. The alleged activities spanned from 2017 through 2025, covering a period of eight years. The case highlights systemic weaknesses in monitoring mining permits. Tan’s eventual conviction could serve as a deterrent for similar misconduct.

Industry Implications

For the coal industry, the auction represents a push towards greater accountability and asset recovery. It signals that illegal mining operations will face tangible consequences, potentially prompting stricter compliance among operators. The sale could also affect market supply, as a sudden influx of coal may influence prices temporarily. However, the auction’s transparency may restore confidence among legitimate players. Long-term, the industry may see improved regulatory oversight and better enforcement of licensing.

Investor Considerations

Investors in the energy sector must now reassess risk profiles for companies linked to former operators of PT Asmin Koalindo Tuhup. The auction’s outcome could trigger reassessment of asset valuations in portfolios containing coal holdings. Due diligence will become even more critical, especially regarding compliance history. Firms may seek to diversify away from assets tainted by corruption. The event underscores the importance of aligning investment strategies with regulatory developments.

Regulatory Response

Regulatory bodies responded by reaffirming the eligibility criteria through the Ministerial Decision 162.K/HK.02/MEM.H/2026, clarifying that only legally registered entities can participate. The decision also emphasizes the role of the BPA in ensuring proper management of seized assets. ESDM has pledged to monitor the auction closely, ensuring that the sale adheres to national interests. The collaboration between judicial and regulatory agencies reflects a coordinated approach to combating corruption. This joint stance may encourage other sectors to adopt similar measures.

Community Perspective

Local communities in Barito Selatan have mixed feelings about the auction. While some view it as an opportunity for economic activity, others worry about potential environmental impacts from large-scale coal handling. Residents have expressed concern over air quality and water contamination risks. Community leaders are calling for stricter environmental safeguards during the sale and subsequent use of the coal. The outcome will likely shape public perception of the mining sector in the region.

Future Outlook

Looking ahead, the auction’s proceeds are expected to be earmarked for compensating victims of the illicit operations and supporting local development projects. The BPA may also use the funds to strengthen enforcement mechanisms in the mining sector. Analysts predict that the auction could set a precedent for handling other seized assets across industries. If successful, it could encourage more aggressive asset recovery campaigns. The market will watch closely to gauge the auction’s impact on coal prices and investment sentiment.

Conclusion

In summary, the digital auction of over 237,000 tons of coal marks a significant stride in Indonesia’s fight against mining corruption. By converting seized assets into marketable goods, authorities demonstrate a commitment to transparency and accountability. The event offers both lessons and opportunities for industry participants, investors, and regulators alike. As the process unfolds, stakeholders must remain vigilant, ensuring that the sale upholds the highest standards of fairness. The future of Indonesia’s coal market will undoubtedly reflect the outcomes of this landmark auction.
