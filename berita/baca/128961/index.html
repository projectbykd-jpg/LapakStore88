OJK Benahi Industri Pasar Modal, 129 Pelaku Usaha Masuk Skema Baru

Redaksi LapakStore88 · diolah dari Liputan6 Bisnis

LokalStore88 Kamis,12 November 2026.

On 29 October 2026, a record 192 entities—107 perusahaan efek and 85 manajer investasi—submitted their commitments to OJK, signaling a decisive shift toward stronger governance in Indonesia’s capital market. This unprecedented wave of compliance reflects the regulator’s aggressive push for higher capital adequacy, ownership quality, and risk management standards. For investors, the move promises a more resilient framework where companies are better equipped to handle market volatility. The announcement also hints at a broader strategy to attract foreign confidence and streamline regulatory oversight. As the curtain rises on this new era, the industry watches closely for the tangible effects on trading and investment behavior.

The Otoritas Jasa Keuangan, Indonesia’s financial watchdog, has long championed reforms designed to curb past incidents of misconduct and mismanagement within the market. By tightening capital buffers and enforcing stricter corporate governance, OJK aims to eliminate loopholes that once allowed risky practices to flourish. The recent commitments represent the latest milestone in this long‑term transformation effort. They also underscore OJK’s belief that a robust, transparent market is essential for sustaining long‑term economic growth. In this context, the 192 companies’ adherence is both a compliance check and a public statement of intent.

Central to this initiative are two pivotal regulations issued in 2026: POJK 3, governing the activities of perusahaan efek that act as underwriters and brokers; and POJK 5, outlining the operational scope of manajer investasi. Together, these rules delineate the permissible business activities and establish minimum capital thresholds. They also codify expectations around board composition, internal controls, and disclosure practices. By harmonizing these standards, OJK seeks to level the playing field and reduce asymmetric information. The regulations, therefore, serve as both a roadmap and a yardstick for the industry’s evolution.

Segmenting the Market: PEKU and MIKU

To operationalize the new rules, OJK introduced two activity groups—Perusahaan Efek Kegiatan Usaha (PEKU) and Manajer Investasi Kegiatan Usaha (MIKU). Each group is defined by the complexity and risk profile of the activities the firm intends to pursue. This classification enables regulators to tailor capital and governance requirements to the specific operational realities of each company. It also allows firms to self‑identify their strategic focus before scaling up. The result is a more granular oversight structure that aligns regulatory burden with actual business risk.

Among the 107 PE that filed, a majority opted for the PEKU designation, signaling their intention to engage in underwriting and trading services. A smaller subset chose to operate under a narrower scope, focusing on advisory or niche brokerage activities. This distribution reflects a strategic shift where firms are aligning their operational capabilities with the regulatory thresholds they can comfortably meet. The commitments also include detailed capital adequacy plans, demonstrating a proactive stance toward meeting the stricter thresholds set by POJK 3. These firms now face a clearer roadmap for compliance, reducing ambiguity in future audits.

The 85 MI that submitted their commitments largely fell into the MIKU category, indicating a broad engagement with asset management and portfolio services. A handful of these managers expressed a desire to diversify into alternative investment products, a move that will require additional capital buffers and governance measures. Each MI’s declaration outlines the specific product lines they intend to offer, allowing OJK to assess risk exposure on a case‑by‑case basis. Importantly, these commitments underscore a trend toward greater transparency in product offerings and fee structures. As a result, investors can anticipate clearer disclosures and more reliable performance metrics.

Transition Timeline and Enforcement

OJK has granted a transition window that extends until April 2029, giving firms time to align their operations with the new mandates. During this period, the regulator will conduct regular assessments to verify that capital ratios, board compositions, and risk frameworks are on track. Firms that fail to meet the stipulated conditions will face corrective measures, ranging from additional capital injections to, in severe cases, suspension of certain business lines. The phased approach reflects OJK’s recognition that rapid change can destabilize smaller firms, while still maintaining a firm commitment to market integrity. It also signals the regulator’s willingness to collaborate rather than impose abrupt sanctions.

Monitoring will involve close collaboration between OJK, shareholders, and industry associations. The regulator emphasizes that shareholder engagement is critical to ensuring adequate capital and governance reforms are fully realized. By fostering dialogue between board members and investors, OJK hopes to mitigate conflicts of interest and promote a culture of accountability. Periodic reports will be required, detailing progress on capital adequacy, internal controls, and compliance training. These disclosures are intended to keep the market’s pulse visible and to reassure both domestic and foreign stakeholders.

One of the key lessons from past scandals is the necessity of robust capital reserves. OJK’s insistence on higher equity levels is designed to act as a buffer against market downturns and to prevent companies from compromising on ethical standards for short‑term gains. In addition, the regulator is encouraging firms to adopt international best practices in corporate governance, including independent audit committees and whistle‑blowing mechanisms. Such measures aim to reinforce trust among investors who may otherwise be wary of opaque decision‑making. Ultimately, a solid capital base supports sustainable growth and long‑term investor confidence.

Beyond capital, OJK is tightening internal controls and compliance functions. Firms are now required to establish dedicated compliance units that oversee adherence to all regulatory obligations. These units must conduct regular risk assessments, document findings, and implement corrective actions. Moreover, the regulator expects firms to invest in employee training programs focused on ethics, regulatory awareness, and operational resilience. By embedding compliance into everyday business, companies can preempt violations and foster a culture that prioritizes integrity over short‑term profits.

Enforcement remains a cornerstone of OJK’s strategy. The regulator retains the authority to impose sanctions ranging from fines to license revocation for non‑compliance. In recent instances, OJK has issued penalties to firms that failed to maintain adequate internal controls. Importantly, the regulator also offers a remediation pathway, encouraging firms to correct deficiencies through structured improvement plans. This dual approach balances deterrence with rehabilitation, aiming to create a market environment where compliance is both mandatory and achievable.

In the wake of several high‑profile breaches, OJK has mandated a set of corrective measures for affected perusahaan efek. These include comprehensive staff training, enhanced internal audit procedures, and a full evaluation of the root causes behind the violations. Firms must submit a remediation plan outlining specific actions and timelines. OJK will review these plans and may require periodic updates to ensure sustained progress. The expectation is that such rigorous oversight will reduce recurrence and signal to investors that the regulator takes violations seriously.

Impact on Investors and Market Dynamics

For retail and institutional investors alike, these reforms promise a safer investment landscape. Transparent disclosure and stronger governance reduce the risk of fraud and mismanagement. Moreover, the standardized capital requirements help prevent systemic shocks that could ripple through the entire economy. As a result, foreign investors may view Indonesia as a more attractive destination for capital allocation. However, some market participants worry that the increased regulatory burden could stifle innovation or limit smaller firms’ ability to compete.

Industry stakeholders, from board members to asset managers, largely welcome the regulatory tightening. Many see it as a necessary step to restore confidence after past scandals. Some executives, however, express concerns about the timeline and the additional administrative costs associated with compliance. Despite these reservations, the consensus remains that a healthier market will ultimately benefit all players by fostering fair competition and attracting sustainable capital flows.

Looking Ahead: 2027–2029 Outlook

As the transition period approaches its conclusion, the focus will shift to full implementation of the new standards. OJK plans to intensify monitoring and to refine enforcement mechanisms based on lessons learned during the transitional phase. Firms that have successfully met the requirements by 2029 will enjoy a competitive advantage, while those lagging may face stricter scrutiny. Additionally, OJK hints at future amendments to further align Indonesia’s market with global best practices, including potential integration of ESG considerations. The coming years will test the resilience of the reforms and the market’s capacity to adapt.

In summary, the OJK’s recent push to re‑engineer Indonesia’s capital market marks a watershed moment for the industry. With 192 companies now pledging to upgrade their capital, governance, and risk management, the sector is poised for a more transparent and resilient future. The regulator’s balanced approach—combining stringent enforcement with supportive guidance—sets a precedent for sustainable growth. As investors and firms adjust to the new landscape, the true measure of success will emerge in the stability and attractiveness of Indonesia’s market on the global stage.
